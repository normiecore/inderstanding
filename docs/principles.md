# Principles

These are the rules every part of Inderstanding is held to. They come from one premise: understanding can't be outsourced. An app (or an AI, or a priest, or a horoscope) can hand you a framework, but the meaning only forms when you test it against your own life, over time.

## 1. You do the work. The app supplies conditions for it.

The app offers lenses, questions, structure, time, randomness and mirrors. It never tells you what your experiences mean or what you should believe. When software is tempted to conclude something, it asks a question instead.

## 2. Evidence over belief

Meaning is drawn from what actually happened to you and how you actually responded. Beliefs are treated as working hypotheses: you state them, live by them for a while, and see whether they hold.

## 3. Many lenses, no ranking

Traditions are presented accurately, in their own vocabulary, by the strands their own practitioners would recognise. Nothing is scored as "right". A tradition you were raised in gets the same treatment as one you've never met, and its less-familiar doors (contemplative Christianity, Quaker silence, Kierkegaard, liberation theology) are shown alongside the one you know.

Pushback counts as data. Knowing what you don't believe is part of knowing what you do.

## 4. Time is an ingredient

Some parts unlock by day or by week and can't be rushed. Patterns only show after enough lived material exists. The app is comfortable being slow.

## 5. Outward as well as inward

Alignment shows up in how you treat people. Practices include acts in the world, and the questions include who your choices affect. A philosophy that only improves your mood is incomplete.

## 6. Private by default

Everything stays on your device. No accounts, no analytics. You can export your data as plain JSON and take it anywhere.

## 7. It should not feel like a planner

No checklists, no streaks, no productivity scores, no guilt for missed days. Missing a day means the path waits for you.

## 8. Plain voice

The app speaks like a thoughtful friend who has read widely. No guru tone, no fortune-cookie lines, no slogans.

## Notes on handling traditions

- Prefer a tradition's own key terms with a short plain-English gloss (wu wei, metta, ihsan, middot, ren).
- Quote only what is real and attributable. If paraphrasing, say so.
- Present strands within traditions. "Christianity" alone is too coarse to be useful; the Desert Mothers, Ignatius, the Quakers and liberation theologians read the same moment very differently.
- Be careful with Indigenous and living minority traditions. Prefer named sources speaking for themselves (for example Robin Wall Kimmerer on reciprocity) over generic "ancient wisdom".
- Note popular distortions where they matter (for example, the ikigai Venn diagram is a Western business-blog invention, not a Japanese framework).
