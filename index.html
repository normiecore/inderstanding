Inderstanding

Tools for building your own working philosophy out of your own experiences, then testing it against ordinary days.

Religion, philosophy and even astrology give people a vocabulary and a set of questions. The meaning only forms when you hold your own life up against them and notice what fits. Nobody can do that part for you, including an AI.

These six prototypes lend you other places to stand: other traditions, your future self, the long view, chance. They never tell you what your life means.

Everything runs in your browser and stays on this device. Each tool opens with example content so you can see how it works; clear it when you want to start your own.

The six tools

Ordered by where they sit in the loop.

  • 1 GatherOccasional

    Vantage

    Your experiences become stars. You connect them into constellations and name what they mean, then shift vantage (time, feeling, threads) and watch some figures stretch apart while others form.

    Borrows from star charts and Marcus Aurelius' view from above.

    Open Vantage →
  • 2 Look from elsewhereWeekly

    Prism

    One real moment from your life, read through many traditions and their strands. You mark what lands and what you push back on, and a resonance map builds up from evidence instead of a quiz.

    Includes less familiar doors into familiar traditions, such as the Desert Mothers, Quakers and Kierkegaard.

    Open Prism →
  • 2 Look from elsewhereDaily

    The Question Deck

    The ritual of a tarot or I Ching reading, with every card a question from a tradition instead of an answer. You carry it through the day and answer in a line. The answers become a book you wrote.

    Borrows from Quaker queries, Ignatian examen, koans, poets and philosophers.

    Open the Deck →
  • 2 and 4When a decision comes up

    Council

    Bring a real decision to a table of tradition voices plus personal seats like you at 80 and the person most affected. You rule as chair, and each ruling's principle becomes precedent for the next case.

    Borrows from clearness committees and the way law builds principles from cases.

    Open Council →
  • 3 Try it in the world40 days

    The Long Way

    A 40-day walk with one practice a day from a different tradition, done during your actual day. In the evening you say whether it shifted anything. The ones that did become a Rule of Life in your own words.

    Borrows from pilgrimage routes and the monastic Rule of Life.

    Open The Long Way →
  • 4 State it and test itDaily

    Working Creed

    A few lines in your own words about how you want to live. One surfaces each morning as your bearing, and each evening you note whether it held. Lines that keep drifting get questioned, and every revision is kept.

    Borrows from field geology: your beliefs laid down as strata.

    Open Working Creed →
